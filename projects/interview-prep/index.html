🌸 TRACE cheat sheet: what to know cold

The 20-second pitch

"For about 3 years I was the support contact at TRACE, an AI-powered sports camera company for youth soccer. I handled 15–20+ tickets a day through Zendesk chat and email, helping clubs, coaches and parents with camera hardware, Wi-Fi and app issues. I kept an 88% CSAT, wrote help docs and macros for recurring issues, escalated bugs to engineering, trained new hires and ran QA on cameras before they shipped. Now I want to grow in customer success and support at a software company."

Numbers to remember

  • Apr 2023 – Jun 2026 at TRACE (~3 years)
  • 15–20+ tickets/day (~300–400/month)
  • 88% CSAT
  • Many issues solved in one contact (first-contact resolution)
  • QA on batches of 20+ cameras with Python test scripts
  • 4 years client-facing in total (with Uniqlo)

Who your customers were

  • Youth soccer clubs (admins buying for teams)
  • Coaches recording games and sharing film
  • Parents wanting to watch their kid's highlights

Mostly non-technical people under time pressure (game days). That's why plain-language explanations were your superpower.

Tools you actually used

  • Zendesk: tickets, live chat, email, macros, queue
  • Slack: team + engineering, and pulling up camera info and debug details
  • Google Sheets: QA tracking for camera batches
  • Python test scripts for QA
  • Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Excel
  • Claude + OpenClaw: AI automation

🔑 What to leverage (your strengths)

Hardware + software troubleshooting. You fixed physical cameras, Wi-Fi and the app. Most support people only know software.
Customers under pressure. Game-day deadlines taught you to stay calm and fast.
Documentation. You turned repeat questions into macros and help docs. This is exactly what TeamBuilder wants.
Working with engineering. You reproduced bugs and wrote clear escalation reports.
QA mindset. You caught problems before customers did, so you think about prevention, not just fixing.
Training others. You trained new hires. That's the start of leadership experience.
AI fluency. You built real AI workflows (OpenClaw). Lots of support teams want this right now.
Marketing degree. You understand the customer journey and messaging.

⚠️ Stay honest: how to handle gaps

If they ask about…Say something like
Phone support"Most of my support was chat and email, and I'm very comfortable on video and phone calls. I'd ramp up fast."
Owning accounts / renewals"My role was ticket-based. I didn't own a book of accounts, but I built relationships with repeat customers like club admins and coaches."
SaaS experience"TRACE was hardware plus an app and cloud platform, so I supported software every day: app issues, uploads, accounts and bugs."
Zendesk admin (triggers, reporting)"I used Zendesk daily and built macros. I understand how triggers, automations and SLAs work, and I'd love to go deeper on the admin side."
Team lead experience"I wasn't a formal lead, but I trained new hires on our workflows and ticket standards."
Your official title"My official title was technician, but my day-to-day was customer support and QA." Use this if the background check shows a different title.

Rule of thumb: never claim something you can't explain with a real example. A believable "I haven't done that yet, but here's the closest thing I've done" beats a fake yes.

      📣 SOCi: Sales Development Representative

      Company: SOCi, AI-powered marketing for multi-location brands (Ford, Ace Hardware, Kumon, Liberty Tax). Their "Genius AI" agents handle local marketing work across hundreds of locations: social posts, reviews, local listings, ads. Pay: $50–60K base + commission (you asked for $60K base, OTE flexible). Remote, Miami/Fort Lauderdale preferred. Applied 10/26.
      This is a sales role, not support. An SDR finds and qualifies potential customers, books demos for Account Executives (AEs) and closes small deals. Expect phone-heavy outreach and quotas (meetings booked, pipeline). In the interview, show you're coachable, driven and good at explaining tech, and be honest that sales is a new direction.

      What SOCi sells (in plain words)

      • Who buys it: brands with lots of locations (franchises, retail chains, restaurants, dealerships, tax offices).
      • Their pain: keeping hundreds of local pages, reviews and social accounts updated and on-brand is a ton of work.
      • SOCi's pitch: AI agents do the work of "1,000 local marketers": respond to reviews, post locally, manage listings, so brands get more visibility and engagement without a huge team.
      • Your angle: marketing degree + 3 years explaining an AI product to non-technical customers + you build AI workflows yourself.

      Practice cold call (role-play out loud)

      Pretend you're calling the marketing director of a 200-location restaurant chain. Keep it under 30 seconds before asking a question.

      "Hi Sarah, this is Jason with SOCi. I'll be quick. I work with multi-location brands like Ace Hardware and Kumon. A lot of marketing leaders I talk to say keeping reviews, local pages and social posts current across hundreds of locations eats up their team's time. Is that something you're running into?"

      If yes: "How are you handling it today?" → listen → "Would it be worth 20 minutes with our team to see how our AI agents handle that across all your locations?"

      If "not interested": "Totally fair. Just so I know for the future, is that because you've already got it covered, or it's not a priority right now?"

      If "send me an email": "Happy to. So I send something useful, what's your biggest focus for local marketing this year?"

      If "we already use a tool": "Makes sense, most brands we talk to do. What would you change about it if you could?"
